## Problem Statement

**Goal:** Determine the minimum number of new units that must be acquired each day to keep an army's energy system self-sustaining.

**Setup:**

Each unit, at the moment it is acquired, generates a one-time amount of energy. That energy is split three ways:

- A portion is permanently banked as score and can never be spent again.
- A portion is paid out as a commission to the existing unit that recruited it.
- What remains is net surplus energy, available to fund the system.

Once active, every unit — old or newly acquired — costs a daily upkeep in energy just to remain active. This upkeep is ongoing, for as long as the unit exists.

New units are not acquired using an external budget; they come exclusively from the net surplus energy generated by acquiring units that day.

**Variables:**

| Variable | Description |
|---|---|
| `active_units` | Number of units currently active, at the start of the day |
| `units_acquired` | Number of new units acquired that day *(unknown — what we're solving for)* |
| `energy_generated_per_unit` | One-time energy a unit generates when acquired |
| `score_percentage` | Fraction of that energy permanently banked as score |
| `commission_percentage` | Fraction of that energy paid to the recruiting unit |
| `upkeep_percentage` | Fraction of that energy owed per unit, per day, as upkeep |

**Condition to solve for:**

Find the minimum `units_acquired` such that the net surplus energy generated by the new units — after score is banked and commission is paid — is enough to cover the total daily upkeep owed by all active units (existing + newly acquired) that day. In other words, the system must remain sustainable even after both the score cut and upkeep are paid out.
